## 🎯 Summary Card

The **"New Cadre of Distributors"** under NISM-V-B can sell only **specific simple schemes**: Index Funds tracking widely-followed broad-based indices (Sensex, Nifty 50), large-cap diversified equity funds, gilt funds, FMPs, ETFs (only equity & gold), liquid + ultra-short debt funds. Cannot sell: actively-managed equity (mid/small/sectoral), credit-risk, derivative-based, structured products. Restriction is by SEBI to protect retail with simpler, transparent products.

## 📖 Core Content

### Why a separate cadre

SEBI created the **New Cadre of Distributors** in 2012 to expand MF reach into Tier II/III towns + rural India. These distributors pass NISM-V-B (foundational) instead of the deeper V-A (full distributor exam). To protect retail, they're allowed to sell **only specific simple, transparent schemes**.

### What the New Cadre CAN sell

| Category | Permitted scheme types |
|---|---|
| **Index funds** | Tracking widely-followed broad-based indices: Sensex, Nifty 50 |
| **Large-cap equity** | Plain large-cap diversified funds |
| **Gilt funds** | Government-securities only |
| **FMPs** | Fixed Maturity Plans (close-ended debt) |
| **ETFs** | Equity ETFs (broad index) and Gold ETFs only |
| **Liquid + Ultra-short** | Debt schemes — short tenor, high credit |
| **Money Market funds** | Same family as liquid |

### What the New Cadre CANNOT sell

- **Actively-managed equity** beyond large-cap diversified (mid-cap, small-cap, multi-cap, sectoral, thematic, contra, dividend-yield)
- **Credit-risk debt funds** (require advanced understanding of credit assessment)
- **Hybrid funds** with active asset allocation (Aggressive Hybrid, Dynamic Asset Allocation)
- **International funds / FoFs**
- **Derivatives-based** schemes (Arbitrage)
- **Structured products** / NCDs / market-linked schemes

### Why these restrictions

- **Simplicity:** investor can understand the product in one page.
- **Transparency:** index funds + ETFs have clear benchmark; gilt funds carry only sovereign risk.
- **Lower risk:** no credit risk or active manager risk.
- **Accountability:** mis-selling is harder when product complexity is low.

If a new-cadre distributor wants to sell broader products, they must pass **NISM-V-A** (full distributor exam).

### Index funds + ETFs — the foundational products

| | Index fund | ETF |
|---|---|---|
| Trading | Buy/sell from AMC at NAV | Buy/sell on exchange like a stock |
| Pricing | Once daily (NAV) | Live during market hours |
| Demat required | No | Yes |
| Transaction cost | None (no exchange) | Brokerage + STT |
| Minimum unit size | Fractional allowed | Whole units only |

Both track an index. Index funds suit SIP investors; ETFs suit those wanting intraday flexibility.

### Liquid + Ultra-short funds

- Park short-term cash for **few days to few weeks**.
- Very low credit + interest-rate risk.
- Often used by corporate treasuries for cash management.
- Returns track money-market rates (slightly above SB account; below FD).

### Gold ETFs

- Each unit = 1 gram of physical gold (or fractions).
- Held in demat.
- Lower cost than physical gold (no storage, no making charges).
- Can be sold instantly on the exchange.

### FMPs (Fixed Maturity Plans)

- Close-ended debt schemes with a fixed tenor (e.g. 3 yrs).
- AMC invests in instruments maturing close to the FMP's maturity.
- Investor knows approximate yield at the start.
- Listed on exchange but liquidity is thin — held to maturity typically.

## 🧠 Memory Hooks

- **New Cadre = simple, transparent products only.**
- **CAN sell: Index, Large-cap, Gilt, FMP, equity/gold ETF, Liquid, MM.**
- **CANNOT sell: Mid/Small-cap, Sectoral, Credit-risk, Aggressive Hybrid, International, Derivatives.**
- **To sell broader products → pass NISM-V-A.**
- **Index fund vs ETF:** off-exchange NAV vs on-exchange live; no demat vs demat needed.
- **Gold ETF = 1 unit ≈ 1 gram of gold** (held in demat).
- **FMP** = fixed-tenor debt; predictable yield, listed but illiquid.
