## 🎯 Summary Card

A **derivative** is a contract whose value comes from an **underlying** (stock, index, commodity, currency, bond). Four product types: **forwards, futures, options, swaps**. Three market participants: **hedgers, speculators, arbitrageurs**. Two markets: **OTC** (negotiated, customised) and **exchange-traded** (standardised, clearing-house guaranteed). Six risks: **counterparty, price, liquidity, legal, operational, basis**. Indian derivatives launched on NSE/BSE in **June 2000** with index futures.

## 📖 Core Content

### What is a derivative

A contract with no value in itself — it derives value from something else (the **underlying**). Underlyings span metals (gold, silver), energy (crude, gas), agri (wheat, sugar), and financial assets (shares, bonds, FX, indices).

### Indian timeline (high-yield)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1996 | SEBI's L. C. Gupta committee formed |
| 1998 | J. R. Varma group recommends margining + risk framework |
| 1999 | SCRA amended — derivatives become "securities" |
| **June 2000** | Index futures launch on NSE + BSE (Nifty, Sensex) |
| June 2001 | Index options launch |
| July 2001 | Stock options launch |
| Nov 2001 | Stock futures launch |
| Feb 2013 | MSEI starts derivatives trading |

### Four product types

| Product | Right vs Obligation | Where traded |
|---|---|---|
| **Forward** | Both parties obliged | OTC (customised) |
| **Future** | Both parties obliged | Exchange (standardised) |
| **Option** | Buyer has right, seller has obligation | Exchange or OTC |
| **Swap** | Both parties obliged (series of forwards) | Mostly OTC |

### Three participants

- **Hedger** — has underlying exposure, uses derivative to *reduce* risk.
- **Speculator** — takes a view, uses leverage to *amplify* risk.
- **Arbitrageur** — exploits price gaps between markets, *closes* them.

### OTC vs Exchange-traded

| | OTC | Exchange-traded |
|---|---|---|
| Standardisation | Customised | Standard lot/tenor |
| Counterparty risk | Borne by parties | Clearing corp guarantees |
| Margin | Bilateral | Centrally enforced |
| Disclosure | Private | Public quotes |
| Regulation | Light | Heavy (SEBI) |

### Why derivatives matter

- **Price discovery** based on aggregated views.
- **Risk transfer** — hedgers offload risk to speculators willing to bear it.
- **Shifts speculation** from informal to organised, regulated venues.

### Risks in derivatives

**Counterparty · Price · Liquidity · Legal/Regulatory · Operational** (fraud, bad docs, mis-execution). Plus **basis risk** for hedges (futures price ≠ spot price at unwind). Brokers must give clients a **Model Risk Disclosure Document** at account opening.

## 🧠 Memory Hooks

- **Derivative families = "FFOS"** — **F**orward, **F**uture, **O**ption, **S**wap. Forward + Future are obligations; Option is the only one with right-vs-obligation asymmetry; Swap is a stack of forwards.
- **Three players "HSA"** — **H**edger reduces, **S**peculator takes, **A**rbitrageur balances. Memory cue: "HSA acts like an HSA insurance — reduce, take, balance."
- **June 2000 = Indian derivatives birthday.** Index futures first; options came one year later (June + July 2001).
- **OTC vs Exchange "C-S-C-C-R"** — **C**ustom vs Standard, **S**elf-margined vs Centrally-margined, **C**ounterparty risk on you vs **C**learing corp, **R**ed-tape light vs heavy.
- **5 risks = "CPLLO"** — **C**ounterparty, **P**rice, **L**iquidity, **L**egal, **O**perational. Add **B**asis if hedging.
