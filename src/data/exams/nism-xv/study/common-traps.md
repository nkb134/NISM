# Common Traps for NISM XV

## 1. Sell-side vs Buy-side reports

> "Buy-side analysts publish reports for the public." — **FALSE.** Buy-side reports are **internal-only** (used by the firm's own fund managers). Sell-side reports are public. Independent analysts sell on subscription.

## 2. Modified duration vs Macaulay duration

- **Macaulay** is in years (weighted-avg time to cash flows).
- **Modified** is the % price-change-per-1%-yield-change.
- They're related: **Modified ≈ Macaulay / (1 + YTM)**.
- Don't confuse formulas. Question often gives one, asks the other.

## 3. Bond price + yield are INVERSE

- Yield ↑ → price ↓; yield ↓ → price ↑.
- Higher modified duration → bigger price change for the same yield move.
- Long-maturity + low-coupon bonds = most rate-sensitive.

## 4. EMH versions

- Weak form → past prices priced in → **technical analysis fails**.
- Semi-strong → all public info priced in → **fundamental analysis fails**.
- Strong → even insider info priced in → **even insiders can't beat market**.

Many candidates flip "weak" and "semi-strong". Memorise carefully.

## 5. Top-down vs Bottom-up

- **Top-down = E-I-C** (Economy → Industry → Company).
- **Bottom-up = C-I-E**.
- Both end up validating all three layers; just different starting points.

## 6. Repo vs Reverse Repo

- **Repo** = RBI **lends** to banks.
- **Reverse repo** = RBI **borrows** from banks.
- Repo > Reverse repo (always, in normal corridor).
- MSF is even higher than repo (emergency lending).

## 7. Five Forces — order doesn't matter, but completeness does

The five: **Industry Rivalry, New Entrants, Substitutes, Suppliers, Buyers.** Many candidates miss one or list six. There are exactly five.

## 8. Industry classification (don't muddle)

- **Cyclical** = sensitive to economy → outperforms in EXPANSION.
- **Defensive** = stable demand → outperforms in CONTRACTION.
- "Growth" and "value" are different axes — about future earnings expansion vs current valuation.

## 9. Independent directors thresholds

- **1/3 of board** if executive chair.
- **1/2 of board** if non-executive chair.
- **Audit committee:** ≥ 3 members, **2/3 independent, chair is independent.**
- Stricter than what most candidates remember.

## 10. RPT thresholds

- **Material RPT > 10% of consolidated turnover** → **prior shareholder approval** required.
- Promoter group can't vote on related-party resolutions.
- Disclosure in quarterly + annual filings.

## 11. CFO vs Net Profit

- If **CFO consistently < Net Profit** → earnings aren't converting to cash → red flag (working-capital build-up, aggressive recognition).
- Cash conversion ratio = CFO / Net Profit; target ≥ 1.

## 12. DuPont — three drivers

```
ROE = NPM × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
```

A high ROE could come from any one of these — not necessarily good business quality. **High ROE driven by leverage (high EM)** is risky; high ROE from margin + turnover is sustainable.

## 13. Buyback tax shifted

- **Pre-FY20:** buyback was tax-free in shareholder's hands.
- **Post-FY20:** buyback **tax 23.296% paid by the company**; receipt is tax-free in shareholder's hands.
- Don't apply old rule to new questions.

## 14. DDT abolition

- **DDT (Dividend Distribution Tax) abolished in FY21.**
- Dividends are now **taxable at slab rate** in receiver's hands.
- TDS at 10% if dividend > ₹5,000 per fiscal year.

## 15. WACC + tax shield

- Always use **after-tax cost of debt** in WACC: r_d × (1 − T).
- The pre-tax cost of debt overstates the cost; tax deductibility of interest creates a shield.

## 16. Terminal value dominance

- In a typical DCF, **terminal value > 70% of total** is a warning.
- Be conservative on terminal growth: must be ≤ long-term GDP growth (3-5% typical).
- Sanity-check by using both Gordon + Exit Multiple methods.

## 17. CAPM uses MARKET risk premium, not country premium

- Equity Risk Premium (ERP) = Rm − Rf.
- For India typically 5-7%.
- Don't add an extra "country premium" on top — already baked in.

## 18. Sharpe vs Treynor

- **Sharpe** uses **σ (total risk)** in denominator → for any portfolio.
- **Treynor** uses **β (systematic risk only)** → for **well-diversified** portfolios where unsystematic risk is gone.
- Treynor on a single stock is misleading; use Sharpe.

## 19. Insider trading window

- Trading window closes **T-7 to T+2 days** around results announcement.
- Insiders need a **trading plan** for compliant trading outside this window.
- Penalty: **₹25 cr or 3× profit**, whichever higher.

## 20. RA cooling-off period

- RA cannot trade in a recommended security for ~**30 days before + 5 days after** the recommendation.
- Personal trades need **pre-clearance by compliance**.
- No tipping (sharing recommendation pre-publication).

## 21. NISM-XV is mandatory for RAs

- Both the principal officer AND every employee involved in research must hold NISM-XV.
- Not a one-time tick — must renew via CPE or re-pass before expiry (3 years).

## 22. Net worth: ₹1 lakh individual / ₹25 lakh body corporate

A favourite trap question. Individual is small (₹1 lakh); entity is much higher (₹25 lakh).

## 23. RSI 70/30 — overbought ≠ "sell now"

- RSI > 70 = overbought = momentum is strong upside; **doesn't automatically mean a top**.
- Strong uptrends often keep RSI > 70 for extended periods.
- Use RSI in conjunction with price-action confirmation.

## 24. Bollinger band touch ≠ reversal

- Touching the upper band means price is "stretched", but not necessarily reversing.
- Trend can ride along the band for many sessions.
- Look for price + indicator divergence for reversal evidence.

## 25. Head-and-shoulders is BEARISH (after uptrend)

- Three peaks, middle highest. Break below "neckline" = bearish reversal.
- **Inverse H&S** = after downtrend, three troughs, middle lowest → bullish reversal.

## 26. Contango vs Backwardation

- **Contango**: futures > spot. Normal for storable commodities (carry costs).
- **Backwardation**: spot > futures. Signals tight supply / scarcity.
- Long futures in contango → **negative roll yield** (cost of rolling expiring contracts forward).

## 27. CFA / CIIA / CA does NOT exempt you from NISM-XV

- NISM-XV is mandatory regardless of other qualifications.
- These other certifications may help with research quality; they don't replace the regulatory mandate.

## 28. Chinese walls (information barriers)

- Required between research and IB / proprietary trading desks within the same firm.
- Failure to maintain Chinese walls → enforcement action under SEBI conduct rules.

## 29. SEBI Code of Conduct for RAs (Schedule III)

- Independence + impartiality.
- Honesty + due diligence.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure.
- Confidentiality of non-public client info.
- Compliance with all applicable laws.

Memorise these as a sequence.

## 30. Open offer at 25% stake threshold

- Crossing 25% stake → mandatory open offer for **another 26%** under SAST.
- Goal: ensure other shareholders have a chance to exit if control changes hands.
- Often a tested number in legal-section questions.
