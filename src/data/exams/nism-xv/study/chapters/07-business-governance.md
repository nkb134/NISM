## 🎯 Summary Card

Qualitative side of company analysis. Look for: **moat** (network effects, brand, scale, switching costs, regulatory protection), **business model quality** (recurring revenue, pricing power, capital efficiency), **management quality** (track record, capital allocation, integrity), **governance** (board independence, audit quality, related-party disclosure, ESG). SEBI mandates governance under **LODR Regulations** + **Companies Act 2013**. Red flags: aggressive accounting, frequent auditor changes, related-party leakage, share pledging by promoters.

## 📖 Core Content

### What makes a great business — the moat

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Network effects** | Marketplaces, exchanges, payments |
| **Brand** | FMCG (HUL), luxury (Titan jewellery) |
| **Scale / cost** | Cement, telecom, steel |
| **Switching costs** | Enterprise software, banking |
| **Regulatory** | Licensed industries (banks, insurance, telecom spectrum) |
| **Patents / IP** | Pharma (innovator molecules), tech R&D |
| **Distribution** | FMCG (1M+ outlets), broking (branch network) |

A **deep moat** = sustained competitive advantage → above-cost-of-capital returns over many years.

### Business model dimensions

- **Revenue mix** — recurring (subscription, AMC fees) vs transactional vs one-off.
- **Pricing power** — can the firm pass cost increases to customers? Test: is gross margin stable?
- **Customer concentration** — top 10 customers as % of revenue. >40% = risky.
- **Geographic diversification** — single country / region risk?
- **Capital intensity** — Capex / Sales. High = capital-hungry; low = asset-light.
- **Working capital cycle** — Days sales outstanding + inventory days − days payable. Lower is better.

### Management quality — what to check

- **Track record** — prior roles, business outcomes, scandals.
- **Capital allocation** — ROIC on past acquisitions, dividend / buyback discipline.
- **Tenure** — long-tenure CEO with skin-in-the-game (high promoter holding) usually positive.
- **Communication** — earnings call clarity, transparency in disclosures, no opacity on issues.
- **Integrity** — any past SEBI / SEC enforcement, fraud allegations, accounting restatements.

### Governance framework (India)

- **Companies Act 2013** — board composition, audit committee, related-party transactions.
- **SEBI LODR Regulations** — disclosures for listed entities; separation of Chairman + CEO recommended for top 500.
- **Independent directors** — minimum **1/3 of board** if executive chairman; **half** if non-executive chairman.
- **Audit committee** — at least 3 members, **2/3 independent**, chairman independent.
- **Board diversity** — at least one woman director (top 1000 listed entities).

### Related-party transactions (RPT)

- **Material RPT** = >10% of consolidated turnover or specified threshold.
- Requires **prior shareholder approval** + audit committee scrutiny.
- Promoter group cannot vote on related-party resolutions.
- Disclosure in quarterly + annual filings under LODR.

### ESG considerations

- **Environment** — carbon footprint, water usage, waste, transition risk for high-emission sectors.
- **Social** — employee diversity, supply chain ethics, community impact.
- **Governance** — board structure, executive compensation, anti-corruption controls.
- **BRSR (Business Responsibility & Sustainability Report)** — mandatory for top 1000 listed entities since FY23.

### Red flags

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Frequent **auditor changes** | Possible disagreement on accounting |
| **Qualified audit opinions** | Auditor flagged issues |
| **Promoter share pledging** ↑ | Risk of forced selling on margin call |
| Aggressive **revenue recognition** | Possible inflation of top line |
| **Restated financials** | Past misstatement |
| **Top management exits** | CFO + CEO leaving together = serious |
| **Unrelated diversification** | Distracts from core business |
| **High related-party transactions** | Potential leakage to promoter group |
| **Unusually low effective tax rate** | Possible aggressive tax planning |

### The 'jockey vs horse' question

Some say back the **business** (horse) — a bad jockey on a great horse will eventually win. Others say back the **management** (jockey) — a great manager finds the right business. Best research: **both should be high quality**. If only one, prefer good business; great managers are rarer than great businesses.

## 🧠 Memory Hooks

- **Moat types = "NBSCRPD"** — Network, Brand, Scale, Cost, Regulatory, Patents, Distribution.
- **Customer concentration > 40% = single-customer risk.**
- **Independent directors:** 1/3 if exec chair, 1/2 if non-exec chair.
- **Audit committee:** ≥ 3 members, 2/3 independent, independent chair.
- **Material RPT > 10% of consolidated turnover** — needs prior shareholder approval.
- **BRSR mandatory for top 1000 listed entities** since FY23.
- **Promoter pledge rising = warning sign** (forced-sell risk).
- **CFO + CEO exit together = top-tier red flag.**
