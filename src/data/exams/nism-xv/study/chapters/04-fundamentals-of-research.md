## 🎯 Summary Card

Two big approaches: **Fundamental Analysis (FA)** = study the underlying business; **Technical Analysis (TA)** = study price + volume patterns. FA splits into **top-down** (Economy → Industry → Company) and **bottom-up** (Company → Industry → Economy). Three forms of **Efficient Market Hypothesis**: weak, semi-strong, strong. Behavioural biases distort decisions: anchoring, herding, recency, overconfidence, loss aversion. Quality research = **rigour + objectivity + transparency + reproducibility**.

## 📖 Core Content

### Fundamental vs Technical

| | Fundamental (FA) | Technical (TA) |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Intrinsic value of the business | Past price + volume |
| Inputs | Financials, management, industry | Charts, indicators, patterns |
| Horizon | Months to years | Hours to weeks |
| Question | "Is this stock under/overvalued?" | "Is the trend up or down?" |
| Output | Buy / hold / sell with target | Entry / exit signals |

### Top-down vs Bottom-up FA

**Top-down (E-I-C):** start with the economy, then pick industries with tailwinds, then pick best companies in those industries.

**Bottom-up (C-I-E):** start with a company that looks attractive, then validate it makes sense given its industry + economy.

> Both eventually converge on the same three layers; just different entry points.

### Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH)

| Form | Information reflected | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Weak** | All past prices | Technical analysis can't beat the market |
| **Semi-strong** | All publicly available info | Fundamental analysis can't beat the market |
| **Strong** | All info incl insider | Even insiders can't beat the market |

Real markets show some inefficiency (anomalies persist), so good research can produce alpha.

### Behavioural biases (must-know)

| Bias | Description |
|---|---|
| **Anchoring** | Over-rely on the first piece of information seen |
| **Confirmation** | Seek info that confirms what you already believe |
| **Herding** | Follow the crowd; momentum chasing |
| **Recency** | Over-weight recent events |
| **Overconfidence** | Overestimate own forecasting ability |
| **Loss aversion** | Pain of loss > pleasure of equal gain (Kahneman + Tversky) |
| **Disposition effect** | Sell winners too early, hold losers too long |
| **Availability** | Judge probability by what comes easily to mind |

### Quality of research — what matters

- **Rigour** — clear methodology, audit-trail of sources.
- **Objectivity** — facts over opinions; no advocacy bias.
- **Transparency** — assumptions explicitly stated; calculation shown.
- **Reproducibility** — another analyst can re-derive your numbers.
- **Skepticism** — verify management claims independently.

### Information sources

| Tier | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Annual reports, regulatory filings, RBI / SEBI publications, company management interviews |
| **Secondary** | Analyst reports from peers, industry journals, news media |
| **Tertiary** | Aggregator websites, social media, blog commentary |

Primary > Secondary > Tertiary in reliability.

### The research cycle

1. **Initiation** — pick the target (company / industry).
2. **Data gathering** — financials, peer set, management calls.
3. **Modelling** — DCF, multiples, scenarios.
4. **Conclusion** — buy / hold / sell + price target + risk factors.
5. **Communication** — research report.
6. **Monitoring** — update on results, news, target changes.

## 🧠 Memory Hooks

- **FA looks at the business; TA looks at the chart.**
- **Top-down = E-I-C; Bottom-up = C-I-E.** Same destination, opposite direction.
- **EMH: Weak → Technical fails; Semi-strong → Fundamental fails; Strong → even insiders fail.**
- **Behavioural biases = "AHRO-LDA"** — Anchoring, Herding, Recency, Overconfidence, Loss aversion, Disposition, Availability.
- **Primary sources beat secondary beat tertiary** — always.
