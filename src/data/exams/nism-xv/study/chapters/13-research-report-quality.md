## 🎯 Summary Card

A good research report = **clear thesis + evidence + valuation + risks + disclosures**. Standard structure: **Recommendation upfront** (BUY / HOLD / SELL + target + horizon), then **company snapshot, investment thesis, financials, valuation, risk factors, disclosures**. Independence + transparency mandatory under SEBI RA Regulations 2014. **Conflicts of interest must be disclosed**. Reports are reviewed for compliance before publication.

## 📖 Core Content

### Why structure matters

A reader (institutional or retail) often reads only:
1. The recommendation (1 line).
2. The headline thesis (3-5 bullets).
3. The price target + risk section.

**Bury nothing important.** The first page must communicate the call clearly.

### Standard sections

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Cover** | Company name, ticker, sector, recommendation, target price, current price, market cap, date |
| **Investment thesis** | 3-5 bullets: WHY buy / hold / sell |
| **Company snapshot** | Business model, segments, market share, key customers |
| **Industry context** | Drivers, competitive landscape, growth outlook |
| **Financials** | Historical + forecast P&L, BS, CF; key ratios |
| **Valuation** | Method(s), inputs, sensitivity; comparison with peers |
| **Risk factors** | What could go wrong — quantified where possible |
| **Catalysts / triggers** | What moves the stock to the target |
| **Disclosures** | RA registration, conflicts, distribution restrictions |
| **Disclaimers** | Standard regulatory language |

### Recommendation conventions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BUY / Outperform / Overweight** | Expected return > benchmark + threshold (e.g. +15%) |
| **HOLD / Neutral / Marketweight** | Expected return ≈ benchmark |
| **SELL / Underperform / Underweight** | Expected return < benchmark − threshold |
| **REDUCE** | Mid-bearish; not a full sell |
| **NOT RATED / SUSPENDED** | Coverage paused (often around block deals or pending news) |

Always specify **horizon** (typically 12 months) and **target price**.

### What makes a report excellent

- **Clear thesis** in 3-5 bullets — reader knows the call in 30 seconds.
- **Differentiated insight** — what does this report say that the consensus / sell-side doesn't?
- **Evidence** — specific numbers, dates, quotes. Not vague generalities.
- **Quantified valuation** — show the model; don't just assert "fair value ₹X".
- **Honest risks** — best reports list 3-5 specific risks with potential P&L impact.
- **Track record disclosure** — past calls + their outcomes (optional but trust-building).
- **Plain English** — minimum jargon; explain industry terms briefly.

### Mandatory disclosures (SEBI RA Regulations 2014)

Every report must disclose:
1. **RA's registration number** with SEBI.
2. **Personal ownership** of the subject security (if any) by the analyst or family.
3. **Firm's relationship** with the subject company (advisory, broking, IB, investment).
4. **Compensation** received from the subject company in the past 12 months (if any).
5. **Financial interest / material conflict** of interest of the analyst or firm.
6. **Past recommendations** on the same stock (or a link to them).
7. **Disclaimer** that the report is for information, not investment advice.
8. Statement on **RA's compliance** with the SEBI Code of Conduct.

### Distribution rules

- Reports must reach **all entitled clients simultaneously** — no preferential timing.
- **Embargo until publication** — analyst can't trade ahead of release.
- Cooling-off period typically **30 days** between recommendation issuance and any trading on the analyst's own account in that security.
- **Records retained for 5 years** of all reports + supporting research.

### Common report failures

- **Thesis buried on page 8.** Reader gives up before reaching it.
- **Excessive jargon** — "EBITDA-margin-tailwind-driven re-rating".
- **Cherry-picked comparisons** — peer set chosen to flatter the call.
- **Missing risks** — no balance; reads as advocacy.
- **Vague targets** — "expect material upside" without a number.
- **Stale data** — quoting last year's results when latest quarter is out.
- **Poor charts** — labelless axes, no legend, misleading scales.

### Report types

| Type | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiation of coverage** | 30-50 pages | Comprehensive first take on a name |
| **Quarterly result note** | 4-8 pages | Quick reaction to numbers |
| **Sector / thematic** | 20-40 pages | Top-down view, multi-stock |
| **Company update** | 2-4 pages | Trigger-driven (event, ratings, news) |
| **Daily morning note** | 1-2 pages | Market wrap, highlights |

## 🧠 Memory Hooks

- **Lead with the call.** First page = recommendation + thesis + target.
- **3-5 bullets is the right thesis length.** Longer = no one reads it.
- **Disclose conflicts upfront.** SEBI mandates 7+ specific disclosures.
- **Reports retained 5 years.**
- **Cooling-off ≈ 30 days** before analyst can trade the recommended stock.
- **Distribution must be simultaneous** to all entitled clients.
- **Quantified risks > vague risks.** Best reports name 3-5 specific risks with P&L impact.
- **Initiation = 30-50 pages; quarterly note = 4-8 pages; daily = 1-2.**
